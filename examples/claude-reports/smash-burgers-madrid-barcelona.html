The recommendation

Burgers are one of the fastest-growing formats in Spanish organised dining, with close to 2,800 burger-led chain restaurants operating by March 2026, and the smash style has a following of its own: in a 2024 delivery-platform survey, 30% of Spaniards said they prefer a smash burger to a classic one.

Restaurant-ready premises are plentiful but not cheap in Madrid. The screen returned 692 matches there and 1,604 in Barcelona, with a median ask of €3,000 a month in Madrid and €1,995 in Barcelona. Almost every good option is a business transfer, so key money is the real entry cost.

In Barcelona, take Viladomat in Sant Antoni: the deepest young catchment, a C3 licence that allows late trading, a corner, and the lowest rent per m² on the list. In Madrid, Pradillo and Manuel Becerra are the economic picks, the first an operating restaurant with a terrace and the second the largest delivery catchment of the six. Sagasta at Glorieta de Bilbao is the late-night flagship if the brand needs one, at twice the rent per m².

The smash burger sweet spot

A smash burger kitchen is simple but unforgiving: it needs a griddle line with serious extraction, a licence that already allows cooking, and a young crowd that eats late and orders in.

The smash burger customer

Smash burgers sell to the young. The format grew with delivery after the pandemic and lives on social media, where a crust, a queue and a corner window travel further than any advert. What wins the second visit is consistency: the same burger at 1 am on Saturday as at lunch on Tuesday.

Corner restaurant with C3 mixed licence (restaurant and late-night), 210 m² on two floors, turnkey, terrace for 12, 10-year lease
Lead: Barcelona

The strongest case on the list. Forty thousand people live within a ten-minute walk, the deepest catchment of the six, and 7,300 of them are 15–29. The unit is a turnkey corner with smoke extraction, air conditioning, step-free access and a mixed C3 licence that covers late-night trade, at the lowest rent per m² in the screen.

The catch is the €295,000 transfer fee, which buys the licence, the fit-out and a ten-year lease.

Risks and next steps

Risks and assumptions

  • Most options are business transfers; key money is stated only at Viladomat (€295,000) and Londres (€170,000).
  • Smoke outlet capacity is unknown: a griddle needs more extraction than many bar kitchens have.
  • Barcelona restricts new food licences in parts of the centre through district use plans; check before relying on a new C3.
  • Two Madrid and one Barcelona premises appear in two listings each, with different figures to reconcile.
  • Footfall and demographics are 2024 modelled data; restaurant counts are OpenStreetMap places, under-mapped at Manuel Becerra.
  • Excluded: a Gòtic corner with 1.07 million footfall but 68% visitors, and a Malasaña listing whose text describes another location.

Next steps

  1. Visit Viladomat, Pradillo and Manuel Becerra on a Friday after 9 pm and a Sunday at 8 pm.
  2. Ask for transfer prices, accounts and licence certificates for all six.
  3. Have a kitchen engineer check extraction capacity for a griddle line at the top three.
  4. Model delivery revenue for Manuel Becerra against its 724,000-resident drive catchment.
  5. Run a second Madrid screen in Lavapiés and Malasaña, the densest restaurant supply.
